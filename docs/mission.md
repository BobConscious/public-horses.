# Public Horses mission

**Mission.** Help people find concrete ways to serve their communities and give directly to the people doing the work.

**Vision.** People make service a regular part of life. Community organizers can ask for specific help and get what they need.

The name comes from Geshe Michael Roach’s teaching about willingly sharing our time and resources for others. [Public Horses course](https://www.yogastudiesinstitute.org/offers/LwbhTdqj)

## Participant journey

1. **Ask.** A person or organization makes a specific request. It states what is needed, when, and how to help.
2. **Review.** A reviewer checks the organizer, request, support links, and any image. Approval publishes the reviewed version.
3. **Find and help.** A supporter finds an open request. They give time, skills, supplies, or money through the organizer’s own channel.
4. **Reflect and return.** The supporter can record the help they gave and choose an intention. They can tend the seed in their private garden and find another need when ready.

## Principles

- **Start with a real need.** Use the organizer’s words and current instructions. Put the work and the next action first.
- **Let people move between roles.** A member can organize a project and support another with the same account.
- **Give directly.** Keep the giving relationship between the organizer and the supporter. The listing makes the next step clear.
- **Keep reflection private.** The garden helps people remember how and why they helped. Keep intentions and notes within the member’s account.
- **Make claims the evidence supports.** Treat a report of help as self-reported. Use organizer confirmation when describing verified results.
- **Learn from the people doing the work.** Ask organizers whether the help met their needs. Ask supporters what helped them act or return.

The first proposed pilot starts with up to three Nourishment requests. Fast for Hunger is the proposed first partner. Its meditation can point people to a current need through a Public Horses link. Fast for Hunger owns its registration, reports, and globe. Public Horses owns its directory and garden.
