# Public Horses mission

**Mission.** Help people post and fulfill acts of service, and learn how service shapes the world they want to create.

**Vision.** People make service a lifelong practice. Community organizers can ask for specific help and get what they need.

In the teaching, a village pooled its resources for a horse anyone could use. For yogis, becoming a public horse means spending our lives serving others. The board gives people a way to start. The garden gives them a place to study the karmic correlations of service. They can reflect on what they gave and what they notice in their lives over time. [Public Horses course](https://www.yogastudiesinstitute.org/offers/LwbhTdqj)

## Participant journey

1. **Ask.** A person or organization makes a specific request. It states what is needed, when, how to help, and what counts as complete.
2. **Review.** A reviewer checks the organizer, request, support links, and any image. Approval publishes the reviewed version.
3. **Find and serve.** A supporter responds through the organizer’s own channel. The organizer marks the request fulfilled when the help meets the need.
4. **Reflect and return.** The supporter records what they did and what they want to cultivate. They can return to the private garden to record what they notice and choose another act of service.

## Principles

- **Start with a real need.** Use the organizer’s words and current instructions. Put the work and the next action first.
- **Let people move between roles.** A member can organize a project and support another with the same account.
- **Give directly.** Keep the giving relationship between the organizer and the supporter. The listing makes the next step clear.
- **Keep reflection private.** The garden helps people remember how and why they helped. Keep intentions, acts, and later observations within the member’s account.
- **Make claims the evidence supports.** Treat a garden record as the member’s own account. Use organizer confirmation when describing fulfilled opportunities.
- **Learn from the people doing the work.** Ask organizers whether the help met their needs. Ask supporters what helped them act or return.

The first proposed pilot starts with up to three Nourishment requests. Fast for Hunger is the proposed first partner. Its meditation can point people to a current need through a Public Horses link. Fast for Hunger owns its registration, reports, and globe. Public Horses owns its directory and garden.
